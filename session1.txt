⏺ Bug analysis (Ponytail: trace the real flow first)

  The bug: New customer flow uses a "sentinel id=-1" pattern. The
  create-customer endpoint correctly sets is_new_customer=True and
  new_customer_data in the template context, BUT:

  1. When the user scans a barcode, sales_add_to_basket is called — that
  endpoint receives customer_id=-1 and re-renders the scan step, but it
  explicitly sets is_new_customer=False and new_customer_data=None (line 299-300
  of sales.py). It does NOT pass through the new customer fields.
  2. When sales_remove_from_basket runs, same problem (line 342-343).
  3. The form on the scan step only renders the new_* hidden fields when
  is_new_customer and new_customer_data (line 200 of checkout.html), so the
  new_phone etc. never get posted to confirm-sale. Then if not new_phone
  triggers the error.

  Root cause: The scan-step renderings lose the new-customer data. The data is
  only in server-side context, and it gets clobbered on every re-render of the
  scan step.

  Lazy fix (Ponytail ladder rung 1-2): Don't use a "sentinel id=-1" + re-render
  dance at all. Save the customer as soon as create-customer is submitted (it's
  a one-step flow — entering name/birthday implies commitment). Then the scan
  step has a real customer.id. This eliminates the entire "is_new_customer"
  plumbing, fixes the bug at the root, and shortens the code.

  Let me apply this fix. It's a one-line conceptual change with a small diff.

  brand "رای کیدز" (Ray Kids). Audience: shop staff. Job: scan, charge, and
  grow customer loyalty through referrals. Subject's world is children's

  Design plan (Pass 1):

  Subject grounding: Children's clothing shop. Persian/RTL. The world of the
  store is colorful, tactile, hand-illustrated, parental. Most generic AI
  designs for Persian apps reach for pink/purple gradients (this app already has
  #ff6b9d → #c44dff — that's the default trap, and it shows on the current
  site). I'll move the brand away from candy-pastel into a warm, hand-printed, 
  paper-and-ink aesthetic — think greeting card shop meets corner store — so the
  checkout experience feels distinct, not like a kids' birthday invite.

  Palette (4–6 hex, deliberate):
  - #1F1B16 Ink (text, primary)
  - #F5EFE3 Paper (background, soft cream)
  - #E27D3B Persimmon (primary accent — the signature warm orange)
  - #2E5D4F Pine (secondary — used for confirm/success)
  - #C8A464 Saffron (tertiary for badges/tiers)
  - #A4303F Pomegranate (danger/refund)

  Typography:
  - Display: Vazirmatn already loaded — keep, it's the Persian standard and the
  right call. Tighten letter-spacing and use it at a confident 700/800.
  - Numerals: enable font-feature-settings: "tnum" so prices align in the
  basket.
  - Scale: 12 / 14 / 16 / 20 / 28 / 40. Use 40 only for the hero phone-input
  moment; everything else stays inside the 14–28 range.

  Layout concept: a till receipt. The whole checkout is laid out like a long
  printed receipt — center-aligned numerals, ruled dividers, monospace-style
  data. This is the signature: a real, tactile artifact, not a generic POS card
  grid.

  Signature element: the phone-input screen renders as a giant paper receipt 
  header with perforated edge and an embossed logo, then the workflow continues
  as a receipt body. Subtle paper texture, hairline rules, no border-radius on
  surfaces. Tier badges become stamps (slight rotation, ink-bleed shadow) rather
  than gradient pills.

  Anti-default checks against the brief's trap set: I'm explicitly NOT using
  #F4F1EA cream + serif (it's a habit trap, but the receipt concept earns it as
  a deliberate choice, not a default), NOT using near-black + acid green, NOT
  using numbered 01/02/03 markers. The signature is the till-receipt metaphor,
  applied consistently through every page.
